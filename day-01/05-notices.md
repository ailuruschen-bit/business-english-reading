# Unit 05 · Notices, Construction and Customer Service

> **Gaps this unit targets**: `commuter` / `ongoing construction` / `undergoing construction` / `the inconvenience that the delay is causing passengers` / `take place vs. take the place of` / `prompt / promptly` / `confirmation call` / `go off` / `on schedule` / `drop by` / `be excused` / `extend business hours` / `cater to both A and B` / `follow a procedure` / `in this respect` / `courteous` / `only a phone call away` / `numerous times` / `It just so happens that`
>
> **Time**: 10 minutes

## Passage A — Transit Notice

**METRO TRANSIT AUTHORITY — Service Advisory**

**Effective Monday, 6 October: Weekday Delays on the Green Line**

Because of **ongoing construction** at Calder Junction, Green Line trains will operate at reduced frequency between 9:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. on weekdays. The pedestrian bridge at the north entrance is also **undergoing construction** and will remain closed until spring.

**Commuters** travelling during peak hours should not be affected. Passengers who normally travel mid-morning should allow an additional fifteen minutes.

The Authority regrets **the inconvenience that these delays are causing passengers** and thanks riders for their patience. Shuttle buses will **take the place of** trains on the Calder–Westfield segment; the replacement service will **take place** from the surface stop on Weir Street, not from the platform.

Should there be any further changes to the schedule, we will notify riders **promptly** through the Authority's website and station displays. Station staff are **only a phone call away** at 1-800-555-0144 and will be happy to assist.

*Please note: bicycles may not be carried on shuttle buses. Riders are asked to **dispose of** all litter **properly** before boarding.*

## Passage B — Customer Service Email

Dear Ms. Bergström,

Thank you for your message of 2 October regarding your stay at the Harbourview.

I have reviewed your file and can confirm that our reservations team attempted to reach you by telephone **numerous times** on the afternoon of 28 September. Our standard **procedure** is to place a **confirmation call** twenty-four hours before arrival; when a guest cannot be reached, the room is held until 6:00 p.m. only. **It just so happens that** a conference block had filled the hotel that week, and the room was released at six.

I understand this explanation does not undo your inconvenience, and **in this respect** I would like to offer a complimentary night's stay, valid for twelve months.

I should add that our front-desk team has been **courteous** and professional throughout this matter, and I have asked them to **cater to** both your dietary requirements and your preference for a quiet floor should you choose to return.

Our restaurant has recently **extended its business hours** to 11:00 p.m., and the fitness centre now opens at 5:30 a.m. If you are ever in the area, please feel free to **drop by** — no reservation is necessary.

Kind regards,
Priya Raghunathan, Guest Relations Manager

## Notes

### 1. `ongoing` vs. `undergoing` — similar shapes, different grammar

Your deck flags this pair, and it is a Part 5 regular:

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ongoing` | **adjective** | continuing, in progress | **before** a noun: *ongoing construction* |
| `undergo` | **verb** | to experience, to be subjected to | subject **is undergoing** something |

Compare:

> Delays are caused by **ongoing construction**. ✅ adjective + noun
> The bridge **is undergoing construction**. ✅ the subject is experiencing it

⚠️ Wrong: <s>The bridge is ongoing construction.</s> — an adjective cannot carry the predicate this way.

`undergo` is irregular: **undergo – underwent – undergone**.
Collocations: `undergo renovation / repairs / surgery / a transformation / testing`.

Also note that modern spelling makes `ongoing` **one word**, not <s>on-going</s>.

### 2. `take place` vs. `take the place of` — one *the* changes everything

Another pair from your deck:

| Phrase | Meaning | What the subject is |
|---|---|---|
| `take place` | **to happen, to be held** | an **event** |
| `take the place of` | **to replace** | a **person or thing** |
| `replace` | to replace (transitive verb) | a person or thing |

> The ceremony will **take place** on Friday. — it will be held
> Shuttle buses will **take the place of** trains. — they will replace the trains
> = Shuttle buses will **replace** trains.

⚠️ `take place` **has no passive form** — it already means "to happen":
<s>The meeting was taken place.</s> ❌

Passage A puts both phrases in one paragraph on purpose, so you have to separate them in live context.

### 3. `commuter` / `rider` / `passenger`

- **`commuter`** = **someone who travels regularly to work**. Verb `commute`; the noun can also mean the journey itself (`a two-hour commute`).
- **`rider`** = a passenger on public transport (American usage).
- **`passenger`** = a passenger of any kind.
- **`peak hours` / `rush hour`** = the busiest travel times | `off-peak` = outside them.

A Part 7 detail trap: the notice says "Commuters travelling during **peak hours** should not be affected," so "Who is most likely to be affected?" → **people travelling mid-morning**, because the reduced service runs 9:00–3:00, which falls between the two rush hours. **With any timetable notice, pair each time window with the group it affects as you read.**

### 4. `the inconvenience that the delay is causing passengers` — a double-object clause

This phrasing recurs throughout TOEIC apologies and is worth taking apart:

```
the inconvenience [that the delay is causing passengers]
                   └─ relative clause; "that" is the direct object of "causing"
                      cause + person + thing = to cause someone something
```

`cause sb sth` takes two objects, and can also be written `cause sth to sb`.

The standard apology formulas, worth learning whole:
- `We apologize for any inconvenience caused.`
- `We regret the inconvenience this may cause.`
- `Thank you for your patience during this period.`
- `considerable inconvenience` = significant disruption (in your deck)

⚠️ `inconvenience` is normally **uncountable**: `any inconvenience` ✅, not <s>an inconvenience</s> in formal written English.

### 5. `prompt` — adjective, adverb and verb are all tested

This word appears twice in your deck (`prompt` and `promptly`), so it is a genuine sore spot:

| Form | Part of speech | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `prompt` | adjective | **quick, immediate** — *a prompt reply* |
| `prompt` | adjective | **punctual** — *Please be prompt during meal times.* |
| `prompt` | verb | **to trigger, to give rise to** — *The complaint prompted a review.* |
| `promptly` | adverb | ① immediately ② **exactly (on the hour)** |

⚠️ Those two adverb senses are separated only by context:
- `Please notify us **promptly**.` = **without delay**
- `The gala will commence **promptly** at seven.` = **at seven sharp**

Near-synonyms: `prompt` is about speed; `punctual` is about not being late.
`promptly` ≈ `immediately` ≈ `without delay` ≈ `at once`.

### 6. `go off` / `on schedule` / `drop by` / `be excused`

- **`go off`** = for an alarm, **to sound** (also: to explode; British: for food to spoil)
  > The alarm system will **go off** if someone tries to enter after hours.
  The sense is counter-intuitive — an alarm "goes off" when it *starts*, because the original idea is *to be triggered*.
- **`on schedule`** = **on time, as planned**
  > The automobile parts arrived **on schedule**.
  Compare `ahead of schedule` (early), `behind schedule` (late), `on time` (more colloquial).
- **`drop by`** = `drop in` = `stop by` = **to visit briefly and informally**
  > I can **drop by** this afternoon.
- **`be excused`** = **to be permitted to leave, or to be released from an obligation**
  > After dinner, may I **be excused**? — a polite request to leave the table
  > Students may be **excused from** the final exam. — exempted

### 7. `cater to` / `courteous` / `procedure` / `in this respect`

- **`cater to sb/sth`** = **to serve or accommodate** a need; `cater **for** an event` = to supply food for it.
  > We cater **to** both business travellers and families.
  ⚠️ With `both … and …`, the two items must be **structurally parallel** — in Passage B, *your dietary requirements* and *your preference* are both noun phrases. That parallelism is exactly what Part 5 tests.
- **`courteous`** [ˈkɜːrtiəs] = **polite and considerate**, more formal than *polite* and more reserved than *friendly*. Noun `courtesy`.
  `courtesy` also carries a "provided free" sense: `a courtesy shuttle`, `a courtesy call`.
- **`procedure`** = **an established set of steps**. `follow a procedure`.
  `standard procedure`, `safety procedures`.
  ⚠️ Keep three relatives apart: `proceed` (verb, to continue), `procedure` (noun, a process), `proceeds` (noun, money raised — see Unit 02).
- **`in this respect`** = **on this point, in this regard**.
  Near-synonyms: `in this regard`, `on this point`.
  ⚠️ In the sense of "aspect," `respect` is countable: `in many respects`. It is the same word as *respect* (esteem); collocation tells you which.

### 8. `It just so happens that…` — a colloquial "as it turns out"

> **It just so happens that** a conference block had filled the hotel that week.

= **as it happens, coincidentally** — stronger than plain `happen to`, and often carrying a note of unfortunate timing.

Relatives:
- `It happens that…`
- `S + happen to do` — *I happened to be in the area.*
- `as it happens` (parenthetical)

**Reading the context**: in a service email, this phrase usually introduces **a coincidence that worked against the customer** — here, why the room was released. Part 7: "Why was the room not held?" → the hotel was full because of a conference block, and the guest could not be reached.

### 9. `numerous times` / `only a phone call away` / `extend business hours`

- `numerous times` = **many times** (`numerous` is the formal register of *many*)
- `only a phone call away` = **easily reachable by phone** — a figurative use of *away* for distance
  Same pattern: `help is just a click away`
- `extend business hours` = **to stay open longer** (compare `extend employment to` in Unit 01 — this is the literal sense of the verb)

## Self-test

**1.** Choose: The historic bridge is currently ______ extensive renovation.
(A) ongoing  (B) undergoing  (C) going off  (D) taking place

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(B) undergoing**. The bridge *is experiencing* renovation, so you need a **verb**.

(A) is an adjective and cannot form the predicate this way. (D) `take place` requires an event as its subject and is intransitive, so it cannot take *renovation* as an object.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite using *replace*: Shuttle buses will take the place of trains.

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

→ Shuttle buses will **replace** trains.

⚠️ Keep the direction straight: *A takes the place of B* = *A replaces B* = **A substitutes for B**. Reversed, it becomes *B is replaced **by** A*. TOEIC paraphrase questions love to flip this.

</details>

**3.** Choose: The ceremony will ______ in the main auditorium.
(A) be taken place  (B) take place  (C) take the place  (D) be held place

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(B) take place**.

`take place` is intransitive and **has no passive**, so (A) is impossible. If you want a passive, change the verb: `will **be held** in the main auditorium` ✅

</details>

**4.** According to Passage A, which passengers are most likely to be affected?

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Those travelling between 9:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m.** — the notice names "Passengers who normally travel mid-morning."

Peak-hour commuters are explicitly said to be unaffected. This is the classic elimination-style detail question: the text tells you who is **not** affected, and the answer lies among those who remain.

</details>

**5.** In Passage B, what compensation is offered, and what limits it?

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Compensation**: one complimentary night's stay.
**Limit**: valid for twelve months.

`complimentary` = **free of charge** — a high-frequency item, and ⚠️ not to be confused with `complementary` (completing something else).
- `complimentary breakfast` = a free breakfast
- `complementary colours` = colours that complete each other

The two differ by a single letter (**i** vs **e**), and TOEIC will put them in the same set of Part 5 options. Hook: compl**i**mentary contains **i**, as in "**I** pay nothing."

</details>
