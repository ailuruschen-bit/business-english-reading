# Unit 01 · Hiring and Employment

> **Gaps this unit targets** (drawn from your own deck): `applicant` / `apply & application` / `opening` / `eligible for` / `full benefits` / `diploma` / `demeanor` / `punctual` / `stellar` / `possess a keen eye for detail` / `outgoing` / `have a good command of` / `extend employment to` / `subsequently` / `resignation` / `work to deadlines`
>
> **Time**: 10 minutes (3 reading, 5 notes, 2 self-test)

## Passage A — Job Posting

**Northbridge Media Group — Production Assistant (Full-Time)**

Northbridge Media Group currently has two **openings** for Production Assistants in our Pre-Press Department. Both positions became available following the **resignation** of two long-serving staff members, and we are seeking replacements who can begin work on or before 15 October.

The successful **applicant** will report directly to the Production Manager and will work **extensively** with the design and editorial teams. Duties include preparing files for press, verifying colour accuracy, and maintaining the department's archive of finished artwork.

**Requirements:**

- A diploma or degree in graphic design, print production, or a related field
- At least two years' experience in a **press ad design** environment
- A good command of at least one industry-standard layout application
- A keen eye for detail and the ability to work to tight deadlines
- An outgoing **demeanor** and the confidence to deal with clients directly

Employees are **eligible for** full benefits, including comprehensive medical insurance, after a probationary period of six months. Salary will be commensurate with experience.

Candidates should apply in writing, enclosing a résumé and three samples of recent work. Applications received after 30 September will not be considered. Only those selected for an interview will be contacted; we regret that we cannot respond to every **application** individually.

## Passage B — Cover Letter

Dear Ms. Alvarado,

I am writing to apply for the position of Production Assistant advertised in the 12 September edition of the *Regional Business Weekly*.

I hold a diploma in Print Media from Barton College and have spent the past three years at Kestrel Print, where I was **subsequently** promoted from junior operator to shift supervisor. In that role I was responsible for scheduling, for quality control, and for ensuring that every job left the plant **on schedule**.

Colleagues would describe me as **punctual**, methodical, and comfortable working under pressure. I possess a keen eye for detail — a quality that has served me well in a department where a single misaligned colour plate can cost a client thousands.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience might benefit Northbridge Media Group, and I am available for an interview at your convenience.

Yours sincerely,
Daniel Whitfield

## Notes

### 1. `opening` — a job vacancy, not an aperture

In TOEIC the dominant sense of `opening` is **a job opening = a vacant position**. Countable.

> The hospital has two job **openings** for certified X-ray technicians.

The synonym ladder (Part 7 paraphrase questions swap freely among these):

| Term | Register |
|---|---|
| `opening` / `job opening` | Most common, neutral |
| `vacancy` | British, formal |
| `position` | Neutral; the post itself |
| `post` | British, formal |

⚠️ Watch the other use of `vacant`: `a shortage of **vacant** office space` means *unoccupied space*, not a job.

### 2. `apply` / `application` / `applicant` — one root, three traps

- **`apply for + a job`** = to apply for. **`apply to + an institution or person`** = to apply to.
  - `apply **for** the position` ✅ | `apply **to** the university` ✅
  - Common error: <s>apply the position</s> (dropped preposition)
- **`application`**: ① a formal request ② a piece of software (`layout application`). Both senses appear in TOEIC Part 7.
- **`applicant`**: the person applying. The `-ant` suffix marks a person — compare `consultant`, `accountant`.

One more high-frequency sense: `apply` can also mean **to be relevant to**.
> This offer does not **apply to** existing members.

### 3. `be eligible for` — to qualify for a benefit

> Employees may be **eligible for** additional benefits after one year.

Fixed patterns: `be eligible **for** + noun` / `be eligible **to** + verb`.

A three-way distinction TOEIC loves:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `eligible` | **Meets the formal conditions** | eligible for a pension |
| `qualified` | **Has the skills or credentials** | a qualified accountant |
| `entitled` | **Has a legal or contractual right** | entitled to two weeks' leave |

### 4. `full benefits` / `comprehensive medical insurance`

In a workplace context `benefits` (plural) means **employment benefits**, not "advantages."
- `full benefits` = the complete benefits package
- `benefits package` = the whole set of non-salary compensation
- `comprehensive medical insurance` — here `comprehensive` means *covering everything*

⚠️ Do not confuse with `benefit from` (to gain from). Both can appear in the same passage.

### 5. `demeanor` — outward manner

> An **outgoing demeanor** and the confidence to deal with clients directly.

`demeanor` (British `demeanour`) = the way a person outwardly behaves or carries themselves. Uncountable. In job ads it travels with a small set of adjectives:

- `a professional / calm / pleasant / outgoing demeanor`

The adjectives that cluster around it:

| Word | Meaning | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| `outgoing` | sociable, extroverted | also "departing": *the outgoing manager* |
| `punctual` | on time | *punct-* = point → right on the dot |
| `stellar` | outstanding | *stella* = star |
| `courteous` | polite (formal) | more formal than *friendly* |
| `diligent` | hard-working | *with diligence and cooperation* |

⚠️ Both senses of `outgoing` are tested. Let context decide: `outgoing personality` (sociable) vs `outgoing chairman` (leaving office).

### 6. `a keen eye for detail` / `a good command of`

Learn these as whole blocks — never assemble them word by word:

- `possess / have a keen eye for detail` = to be highly attentive to detail
- `have a good command of + language or skill` = to be proficient in
  - `a good command of English` / `a good command of the software`
- `work to deadlines` = to work within fixed time limits (note `to`, not <s>work on deadlines</s>)
- `work under pressure` = to cope with stress

### 7. `subsequently` — afterwards

> I was **subsequently** promoted from junior operator to shift supervisor.

`subsequently` = later, afterwards, but more formal. Part 5 often asks you to pick among similar-looking adverbs:

| Adverb | Logic |
|---|---|
| `subsequently` | *Time*: after that |
| `consequently` | *Cause*: therefore |
| `presumably` | *Inference*: it is assumed |
| `indifferently` | *Manner*: without interest |

⚠️ Your deck pairs `indifferently` with `presumably`. They look related and are not: `indifferent` = not caring (*in-* + *different* → "it makes no difference to me"); `presumably` = it can be presumed.

### 8. `extend employment to sb` — to make a formal job offer

> The company decided to **extend employment to** three of the five finalists.

In formal business English `extend` often means **to offer or grant**, not to lengthen:

- `extend employment to sb` = to hire someone
- `extend an invitation to sb` = to invite
- `extend my sincere condolences to sb` = to offer sympathy
- `extend business hours` = to stay open longer ← this one *is* the literal sense

**One root, two lines.** When you meet `extend`, check what follows: a span of time or space (literal lengthening), or a person plus something offered (formal giving).

## Self-test

**1.** Fill the gap: The hospital has two job ______ for certified X-ray technicians.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`openings`. Plural. `vacancies` also works, but `openings` is the standard TOEIC wording.

</details>

**2.** Choose: Employees are eligible ______ full benefits after six months.
(A) to  (B) for  (C) with  (D) of

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(B) for**. `be eligible for + noun`. With a verb you would need `to`: *eligible **to** receive full benefits*.

</details>

**3.** Which sentence is wrong?
(A) She has a good command of Japanese.
(B) He possesses a keen eye for detail.
(C) We need someone who can work on deadlines.
(D) The outgoing chairman gave a farewell speech.

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(C)**. The fixed collocation is `work **to** deadlines`, or `meet deadlines`.

(D) is fine — `outgoing` here is the second sense, "leaving office."

</details>

**4.** Passage A says "Only those selected for an interview will be contacted." What will an applicant who submitted on 28 September but was not shortlisted receive?

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Nothing at all.** The ad states that only shortlisted candidates are contacted and that the company cannot respond to every application individually.

Watch the date trap. 28 September is *before* the 30 September cut-off, so the application was valid — it simply was not selected. Had the question said 2 October, the answer would change to "it would not be considered." TOEIC habitually builds two layers of judgement into a single date.

</details>
