## Passage A — Invitation

**THE MERIDIAN ARTS COUNCIL**
*requests the pleasure of your company at our*
**Inaugural Gala Celebration**

Saturday, 18 October · 7:00 p.m.
The Ashworth Rooms, 44 Calder Street

Please join us as we mark twenty-five years of service to the region. The evening will **commence** promptly at seven with a reception in the west gallery, after which the Council's Chair will **propose a toast to** our **distinguished guests**. Dinner will be served at eight, and the curtains go up on our anniversary programme at nine.

A **special feature** of the evening will be a short recital by three **luminaries** of the regional music scene, two of whom began their careers on Council scholarships.

All **proceeds from this event** will be contributed to the continued operation of the Council's community studios.

Tickets: $120 per person, or $1,100 for a table of ten.
**RSVP by 4 October** to events@meridianarts.org.

*Formal dress. Complete dinners are included; the cash bar is excluded from the ticket price.*

## Passage B — Trade Show Notice to Exhibitors

**BAY CITY EXPOSITION CENTRE — Notice to Exhibitors, Autumn Auto Show**

Exhibitors are reminded that move-in begins at 6:00 a.m. on Thursday. The Centre will supply **partitions** and **lecterns** to all booths in Halls B and C at no additional charge; **pop-up displays** exceeding 2.4 metres in height must be approved by the floor manager in advance.

Our company has been organizing **conventions** for the last fifteen years, and we ask exhibitors to observe the following:

1. All packing material must **be disposed of properly** in the designated bays. Exhibitors who leave crates in the aisles **may be subject to** a clean-up charge.
2. Electrical equipment found to be **malfunctioning** will be disconnected by Centre staff without notice.
3. All equipment supplied by the Centre must **remain Centre property** after the event.
4. Exhibitors must give **24 hours' notice** of any intention to alter their stand layout.

Please note that the Small Business **Seminar** originally scheduled for Friday morning will now take place on Friday afternoon, **as was first planned**. **Due to popular demand**, a second **introductory session** has been added at 4:00 p.m.

## Notes

### 1. The four event words: `seminar` / `conference` / `convention` / `exposition`

A perennial reading point, and one your deck flags explicitly. The difference is **scale** plus **purpose**:

| Word | Scale | Purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `seminar` | Smallest, dozens | **Instruction**, interactive | attend a seminar on marketing |
| `lecture` | Small, one-way | **One speaker, an audience** | give a lecture |
| `conference` | Medium to large | **Peer exchange**, many sessions | an academic conference |
| `convention` | Large | **An industry or organisation's annual gathering** | the annual sales convention |
| `exposition` (`expo`) | Large | **Displaying products**, with booths | the auto show, a trade exposition |

Hooks:
- `sem-` → think *seed*: small-scale, planted knowledge
- `con-vene` = come (*vene*) together (*con*) → everybody shows up
- `ex-pos-ition` = put (*pos*) out (*ex*) → put on display

⚠️ Your card `lecture vs. conference / as part of` is really testing **`as part of`**:
> The lecture is offered **as part of** the three-day conference.

The two are not parallel; one **contains** the other. The reading section asks "The lecture is…?" and the answer is *one event within a larger conference*.

One more phrase from your deck: `large, organized affairs`. Here `affair` means **an organised event or occasion** — not a matter of business, and certainly not a romance.
> Conventions are large, organized **affairs** that require months of planning.

### 2. Trade show hardware: `partition` / `lectern` / `pop-up display` / `booth`

These words appear only in exhibition settings — and when they appear, they are usually the answer to a detail question.

- `partition` [pɑːrˈtɪʃn] = **a dividing panel** (what splits a hall into booths). As a verb, to divide up.
- `lectern` [ˈlektərn] = **a stand that holds a speaker's notes**. Not quite the same as `podium` (strictly the platform you stand on), though American usage blurs them.
- `pop-up display` = **a portable fold-out display stand** that springs into shape.
- `booth` = a stand at an exhibition. `stand` is the British equivalent (Passage B uses *stand layout*).

### 3. Gala boilerplate: memorise whole phrases

Invitations are highly formulaic. Learn these as fixed blocks so you can skim past them in the exam:

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `requests the pleasure of your company at` | The most formal way to invite someone |
| `commence promptly at seven` | Begin at seven sharp |
| `propose a toast to sb` | Deliver a toast in someone's honour |
| `raise your glass to sb` | Same idea, less formal |
| `the curtains go up on` | The performance begins |
| `a special feature of the evening` | A highlight of the programme |
| `in celebration of its 25th anniversary` | To mark 25 years |
| `all proceeds will be contributed to` | All money raised goes to |
| `RSVP by 4 October` | Please reply by 4 October |

**`RSVP`** comes from the French *Répondez s'il vous plaît* — "please reply." It works as a verb (`Please RSVP to…`) and as a noun.

**`luminary`** = a distinguished person (*lumin-* = light → someone who shines). Plural `luminaries`. Near-synonyms: `dignitary`, `distinguished guests`.

**`inaugural`** [ɪˈnɔːɡjərəl] = **first-ever**, or **relating to taking office**.
- `the inaugural gala` = the first one ever held
- `an inaugural speech` = an inauguration address
- Verb: `inaugurate`

### 4. `proceeds` / `feature` / `commence` — three words disguised as familiar ones

- **`proceeds`** (always plural, stress on the first syllable, ˈproʊsiːdz) = **the money raised**.
  ⚠️ This is **not** the verb `proceed` (prəˈsiːd, to continue). Same spelling, different words.
  > All **proceeds** from this event will be donated. ✅ noun
  > Please **proceed** to Gate 12. ✅ verb

- **`feature`**:
  - Noun = a highlight, or a feature article
  - Verb = to present prominently, to star
  - **Very common in the passive**: `be featured in` = to be included or showcased in
  > The winning pieces will be **featured in** our Art Book.

- **`commence`** = to begin — considerably more formal than *begin* or *start*, and the default in written notices.
  Noun `commencement` = ① the start ② **a graduation ceremony** (American; this sense is also tested).

### 5. `be subject to` — learn both directions

> Exhibitors who leave crates in the aisles **may be subject to** a clean-up charge.

`be subject to + noun` = **to be liable to, or governed by** — usually with a whiff of unwanted consequence or contingency:

- `subject to change` = liable to change (the small print under a price list)
- `subject to approval` = requires approval
- `be subject to a fine` = may be fined

⚠️ Distinguish `be subjected to` (to be made to undergo), which is stronger: *be subjected to criticism*.

A related item: `be disposed of properly`. Note that **`dispose of` is an inseparable phrasal verb**, so the `of` survives in the passive:
> All packing material must be **disposed of** properly. ✅
> <s>must be disposed properly</s> ❌ ← exactly the point your card "of in dispose of" was marking.

### 6. `as was first planned` — a small, nasty grammar point

> The seminar will now take place on Friday afternoon, **as was first planned**.

Here `as` is a **relative pronoun** referring to the whole preceding clause, and it serves as the **subject** of its own clause. So no `it` may be added:

| Form | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `as was planned` | ✅ *as* is itself the subject |
| `as planned` | ✅ reduced form, the most common |
| `as it was planned` | ❌ two subjects competing |
| `as was first planned` | ✅ an adverb changes nothing |

Same structure: `as was expected`, `as is often the case`, `as we agreed`.

**The logical trap**: `as was first planned` means the schedule has **reverted** to the original arrangement — which implies it was changed at some point in between. The reading section exploits this: "What does the notice suggest about the seminar?" → *Its schedule had been changed previously.*

### 7. `due to popular demand` / `introductory session`

- `due to popular demand` = **in response to widespread interest** — the standard formula for an added date, an encore, or an extension.
- `introductory session` = a beginners' session. Note that `introductory` also means **promotional**: `an introductory offer` is a new-customer discount.

## Self-test

**1.** Choose: The three-day ______ will include twelve seminars, a trade exhibition, and a closing banquet.
(A) lecture  (B) convention  (C) appointment  (D) interview

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(B) convention**. Three days, twelve seminars, an exhibition and a banquet — you need the word with the widest scope. A `lecture` is a single talk; it cannot contain twelve seminars.

</details>

**2.** Correct the error: All packing material must be disposed properly.

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

→ `must be disposed **of** properly`.

`dispose of` is inseparable, so the preposition stays in the passive. Same pattern: `take care of` → `be taken care of`; `deal with` → `be dealt with`.

</details>

**3.** In Passage A, which of the following is **not** covered by the $120 ticket?
(A) The reception  (B) Dinner  (C) Drinks at the bar  (D) The anniversary programme

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(C)**. The italic line at the bottom: `the cash bar is excluded from the ticket price`.

`cash bar` = **a bar where guests pay for their own drinks** (the opposite of an `open bar`, where drinks are free). The word *cash* is half the answer on its own.

Note also `Complete dinners are included` — your card records this phrase with *excluded*. I reversed it deliberately, so that you answer from the text in front of you rather than from memory of the original item.

</details>

**4.** "All proceeds from this event will be contributed to the continued operation of the Council's community studios." What does this tell you about the nature of the evening?

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**It is a fundraiser**, not merely a celebration.

`proceeds` (money raised) + `be contributed to` (donated to) = fundraising. Your deck has a card for `Fulton County Fundraiser` — the same scenario.

Purpose questions often ask "What is the main purpose of the event?" Do not be steered by the heading "Gala Celebration": **the title says celebration, the body says donation, and the answer follows the body.**

</details>
